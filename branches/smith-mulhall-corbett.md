# Alice Mulhall's Washington family — a probable Smith branch

![The Mulhall and Corbett record trail, with the unresolved James Smith bridge](../maps/smith-mulhall-corbett-line.svg)

*Solid links within the older household come from Alice's birth-return and Social Security indexes plus two original censuses. The dashed James-to-Raymond link remains probable for **Ashley's** grandfather: the 1957 marriage-license notice does not name his parents.*

## What the records show

Both [1900](../sources/records/mulhall-dc-household-census-1900.jpg) and [1910](../sources/records/mulhall-dc-household-census-1910.jpg) originals say **John and Margaret were born in DC, and each of their parents was born in Ireland**. If those reports are accurate, *Alice's four grandparents* were Irish-born and their respective families reached the United States before John and Margaret's mid-nineteenth-century births. **No parent names, Irish counties, ships or arrival dates have been verified.** John's 1900 entry reports **May 1853**, while Margaret's reports **January 1859**; the public tree instead labels John **1859** and Margaret **1861**. Use the census ages as contemporary reports, not exact vital dates. The 1894 birth return calls the mother **Maggie Corbett**, and Alice's adult Social Security index calls her **Margaret Corbett**, resolving the surname independently of the tree.

Margaret reported **six children born and four living** in both 1900 and 1910. The four named in 1900 were Eliza, Mary, John T. and Alice; the records do not name the other two or tell when they died. This is a family loss recorded in the household count, without enough evidence to tell a more specific story.

**A work story with limits:** John moved from a metal trade in 1900 to a water-department supervisory job by 1910, as the original sheets describe. This is a real occupational change; it does **not** establish that he worked on any particular reservoir or that the household became wealthier. The 1900 rent entry gives no rent amount.

**How this may relate to Ashley:** Alice's son **James A. Smith** is named with Raymond and Alice in the [1940 census](../sources/records/smith-dc-household-census-1940.jpg). A [1957 original notice](../sources/records/james-smith-gladys-blevins-marriage-license-notice-1957.jpg) joins a **James Allen Smith, 24**, to **Gladys Louise Blevins, 19**, fitting Ashley's grandparents' names and years. The notice does not name James's parents. Until his 1957 civil return, 1990 obituary or death certificate bridges the groom to the DC son, this **Irish-born great-grandparent generation is a well documented branch of a probable ancestor**, not a proven answer for Ashley's immigration date.
