# James Blevins (born about 1761–62): a Revolutionary pensioner's route

![The pensioner's reported route from New England through Virginia to Indiana](../maps/blevins-revolutionary-james.svg)

**Why this James matters:** In a **12 November 1832** Indiana pension application, James Blevens/Blevins said he was born **somewhere in New England**, moved with his father to the area later called **Henry County, Virginia**, while an infant, and moved again to the area later called **Montgomery County** at about age ten. This is a valuable **first-person report of one Blevins family's domestic migration**, not an immigrant arrival record and not a proved connection to Ashley. We read [Will Graves's transcription of pension file S32121](https://revwarapps.org/s32121.pdf); the complete original federal file still needs comparison. [Source note](../research/sources.md).

## What happened at King's Mountain

The [interactive family timeline](../explore/timeline.html#kings-mountain) places this battle alongside other branches' events and opens the cited records for each stop.

On **7 October 1780**, Patriot militia closed around a wooded ridge just inside **South Carolina**. Major **Patrick Ferguson**, a British officer, commanded roughly 1,000 Loyalists on the high ground; the attacking force was a similar size and included Virginians under **William Campbell**. It was largely a fight between American colonists on opposite sides of the Revolution. [NPS battle account](https://www.nps.gov/blri/learn/historyculture/overmountain-men.htm) · [NPS on the two sides and their sparse rosters](https://www.nps.gov/kimo/learn/patriots-and-loyalists.htm).

**Why they converged:** Britain had taken Charleston and defeated a Patriot army at Camden earlier in 1780, then tried to gather Loyalist support across the Carolina backcountry. Ferguson's threat against settlements west of the mountains helped prompt militia leaders to pursue him. Campbell's Virginians joined the *Overmountain* force near **Sycamore Shoals**; other North and South Carolina, Georgia, and piedmont units joined later. About **900 selected mounted men** made the final pursuit via Cowpens, then fought on foot. This is the **documented campaign route**, not a proved itinerary for James personally. [NPS trail history](https://www.nps.gov/ovvi/planyourvisit/park-brochure-part-1.htm).

**What the fighting was like:** The attackers moved up from different sides, using trees for cover and rifle fire. Loyalist bayonet charges drove some groups downhill, but the ring of attackers tightened again. Ferguson was killed and his force surrendered after roughly **an hour**. National Park Service accounts give approximate and somewhat different casualty totals; the important scale is that the Loyalist field force was captured or destroyed, while the Patriot side also suffered deaths and wounds. [NPS battle account](https://www.nps.gov/blri/learn/historyculture/overmountain-men.htm) · [NPS tactics lesson](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/teachers/lessonplans/Reading%20-%20Tactices%20in%20the%20Battle%20of%20Kings%20Mountain.pdf). The defeat disrupted Cornwallis's move through North Carolina and contributed to his withdrawal toward South Carolina; it was one part of a longer southern campaign, followed by Cowpens and other battles. [NPS southern-campaign history](https://www.nps.gov/cowp/learn/historyculture/southerncampaign.htm).

**Where James fits:** James said in **1832** that he fought there and marched with prisoners afterward. His fellow volunteer **John Stone** confirmed that James left **Salisbury, North Carolina, with Campbell's men headed toward King's Mountain**, but Stone stayed behind and could not say from sight that James reached the battlefield. Stone's Salisbury account also differs from the main route reconstructed by the Park Service; the file gives no day-by-day explanation. King's Mountain militia did not keep a complete battle roster, so the pension testimony is unusually useful without settling James's presence beyond doubt. No source places him at a particular spot on the ridge or describes his personal actions in the fight. [Pension S32121, pp. 1 and 4](https://revwarapps.org/s32121.pdf) · [NPS on roster limits](https://www.nps.gov/kimo/learn/patriots-and-loyalists.htm).

The testimony preserves a vivid personal episode: James said he fell ill near **Hanging Rock** while returning from South Carolina and friends eventually took him home. Ellis instead described a **sword cut on James's left hand** and said he saw the scar in 1833. Both statements occur in the pension file; they may describe different parts of his return, but the file does not settle their sequence. [Pension transcription, pp. 1–4](https://revwarapps.org/s32121.pdf).

## Keep the James men separate

This pensioner was a child in the **1760s**. He cannot be the adult **1737 Goochland grantee**. Nor does his account name his father, the **Peach Bottom James** who reportedly died in **1801**, or the **James and John** involved in a **1779 Montgomery insurrection**. His claimed Patriot service therefore cannot be assigned to Ashley's proposed colonial line or used to cancel the separate Loyalist-era account. The [colonial James timeline](blevins-colonial-james.md) keeps those sightings apart.
