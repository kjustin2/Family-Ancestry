# Disqué and Schaaf: mill families in the Palatinate

The [family chart](../research/chart-transcription.md) names Magdalena Disque (born Germany, February 1844) as Matthew Kramer's wife and gives a line back through several Johann Ludwigs to Sebastian Disqué and Anna Barbara Schaaf. **This connecting chain is still chart-sourced.** The specialist [Palatinate miller-family history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BCllerfamilien/litera-d/) corroborates much of the earlier miller cluster, but does not independently connect it to Magdalena in Pennsylvania.

## Documented stories

**A ruined mill rebuilt.** A history drawing on a 1684 Landau council record says Johann Bernhard Disqué offered to rebuild the derelict Kindinger mill at Siebeldingen in return for a temporary break on water dues. Sebastian, identified as his son, became a master miller at Rinnthal. This shows skill and access to operating rights, not aristocratic rank. [Miller-family history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BCllerfamilien/litera-d/).

**Marriage joined two mill networks.** The [Schaaf history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BCllerfamilien/saling-schilling/) records Anna Barbara Schaaf's birth at Geiselberger Mühle on 9 April 1700 and marriage to Sebastian Disqué on 22 November 1718. It names her father as miller Johann/Hans Caspar Schaaf and her mother as **Anna Magdalena, surname unknown**. The old chart instead gives Maria Salome Metzger as mother: a live conflict. Caspar's 1720 fishing right and a 1739 register naming him as miller and listing rent partly in grain suggest a working water-powered grain mill, not ownership of the land. A mill at the site appears in a 1284 record, centuries before this family. [Site history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BChlen-u-m%C3%BChlorte/gau-bickelheim-glan/).

**Three leases, several heirs.** The [Disqué history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BCllerfamilien/litera-d/) reports that Sebastian held three hereditary mill leases at Wilgartswiesen, Rinnthal and Hinterweidenthal, allocated to sons after his death. His son Johann Ludwig bought the Knittelsheim mill around 1768. A contemporary [account copied into the history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BChlen-u-m%C3%BChlorte/kleinottweiler-kusel/) describes two waterwheels, two grinding runs and a hulling run; a 1788 asset inventory names the mill plus fields and meadows. Subsequent operation passed to Johann Jakob and later between brothers in 1839. This is documented **asset succession**, not a demonstrated loss of a fortune.

**A documented Disque migration, with a missing link.** The Institute for Palatinate History's [Magdalena Fröhlich card](https://migration.pfalzgeschichte.de/person/98001) gives her birth on **14 November 1808** at Trippstadt and marriage to **Johann Ludwig Disque** on **10 October 1832** at Wilgartswiesen. It lists seven children: Casimir, Heinrich, Christian Heinrich, Elisabetha, Johannes Ludwig, Margaretha and Philipp—**not the chart's Magdalena Disque**. It says the widowed Magdalena emigrated in **1864** with Christian Heinrich, Philipp, Casimir and Margaretha, citing a Disque research file and *Germans to America*, vol. 16, p. 37. The linked [Margaretha card](https://migration.pfalzgeschichte.de/person/98304) records passage through Le Havre and New York on an index date of 3 December 1864. That is a strong lead for an actual migration story, but we have not viewed the passenger list, and the listed dates should not be read as same-day travel between France and America. The [Johannes Ludwig card](https://migration.pfalzgeschichte.de/person/98258) places a son of the couple in Wilkes-Barre, where he died in 1918. **None of these cards yet proves that the chart's Magdalena was his sister.** The institute warns that its older card data may not meet current research standards.

**A cemetery lead in Wilkes-Barre.** A [PeopleLegacy compilation](https://peoplelegacy.com/magdalena_lena_disque_kramer-2d5Y0N) lists Magdalena “Lena” Disque Kramer (26 February 1844–7 June 1905) at Hanover Cemetery, with Martin Mathias Kramer as spouse and Magdalena Frohlich as mother. It also lists a son Ferdinand L. Kramer (1882–1951). This is a promising set of matches to the chart, **not independent proof**: the page supplies no gravestone or underlying register, duplicates a daughter, and disclaims verification. Cemetery registers, newspaper notices, and church records are the next checks.

## Conflicts and limits

- The chart gives **Philippe Fröhlich and Eleonora Kreb** as parents of Magdalena Fröhlich (born 1808). The [institutional card](https://migration.pfalzgeschichte.de/person/98001) instead names **Casimir Fröhlich and Katharina Rauschel**. The linked parent cards have no original source attached. Treat both parent pairs as competing claims until a baptism or marriage record is inspected.
- The chart's Magdalena Disque was reportedly born in **February 1844**. The institutional [Johannes Ludwig card](https://migration.pfalzgeschichte.de/person/98258) dates the proposed brother to **9 October 1844**. Those dates are unusually close for children of the same mother, another reason to verify both births before accepting the sibling link.
- The chart dates Johann Ludwig Disque (1796–1853) to **7 July 1796–2 January 1853**; the [institutional card](https://migration.pfalzgeschichte.de/person/98000) gives **7 June 1796–22 January 1853**. It names the same spouse and place but cites no original record. His dates remain unresolved.
- The chart gives Johann Ludwig Disque (1745) a November birth; the specialist entry has an April baptism that appears to precede that birth. The chart's spouse names also differ from the specialist's **Maria Barbara Vongerichten**. These may be different people, transcription errors, or conflated families. [Specialist entry](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BCllerfamilien/litera-d/).
- Chart's Johann Conrad (1771–1839) and Johann Ludwig (1796–1853) need church-register proof. A different Johann Ludwig, born 1799, appears in the specialist history.
- The chart places an Anna Maria born circa 1659 in a generation above someone born circa 1625. That is chronologically impossible as printed.
- The specialist history calls the Disqués a Huguenot miller family, but their exact route from France and any proposed medieval noble ancestry need original records. No noble connection is claimed here. [Knittelsheim history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BChlen-u-m%C3%BChlorte/kleinottweiler-kusel/).

For a modern view of the area, see the [Geiselberger mill locality](https://www.pfalz.de/de/kulinarik-in-waldfischbach-burgalben) and the [photographed former Knittelsheim mill](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2026-09-02_Knittelsheimer_M%C3%BChle_(Z5-10899E)_by_Achim_Lammerts.jpg). Modern buildings do not prove what a specific 18th-century ancestor saw.
