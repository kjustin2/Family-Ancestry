# Places, schools, and events

These are documented **settings**, not accounts of what a particular ancestor personally experienced.

## Siebeldingen, Rinnthal and the Palatinate

The [Siebeldingen municipality's school history](https://www.siebeldingen.de/dorfjubilaeum/schule-und-kirche.html) says the 1890 schoolhouse brought previously separate Protestant and Catholic schools under one roof. This suggests why an 18th-century ancestor's education cannot be inferred from today's elementary school. No enrollment, literacy, or school record has yet been found for Sebastian Disqué or his ancestors. A responsible answer to “how was my great-great-great grandfather's elementary education?” must first identify the person, place and childhood years; then seek church, school and local administrative records. Nearby school histories are only comparisons.

In the mill valleys, water power drove grinding equipment and mill rights could include grain rent, water dues and fishing rights. The specific [Geiselberger](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BChlen-u-m%C3%BChlorte/gau-bickelheim-glan/) and [Knittelsheim](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BChlen-u-m%C3%BChlorte/kleinottweiler-kusel/) records anchor that context far better than a generic picture of “old Germany.”

## Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania

The [city directories](https://pagenweb.org/~luzerne/towns/donnacd.htm) place candidate Kramers on Susquehanna and Prospect Streets across 1871–1914. These are **historical addresses**; a modern map pin or surviving building should not be treated as the original dwelling without parcel research. Wilkes-Barre's wider anthracite economy is documented by [Pennsylvania's mining history](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/dgs/documents/publications/documents/the%20pennsylvania%20manual%20volume%20127.pdf), but no record found here calls Matthew or Ferdinand a coal miner.

The [1889 directory](https://pagenweb.org/~luzerne/towns/donnacd.htm) lists Kate Kramer at 22 Susquehanna as a silk-mill beamer. [Pennsylvania Heritage](https://paheritage.wpengine.com/article/if-looms-could-speak-story-pennsylvanias-silk-industry/) explains how northeastern Pennsylvania silk mills employed women and girls in the coal region. The listing does not establish Kate's age, school history, wages, or which mill employed her.

Hilda Kramer Wolosz is the one ancestor here with independently reported school attendance: [G.A.R. Memorial High School, class of 1940](https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/citizensvoice/name/hilda-wolosz-obituary?id=7822560). The shared chat also mentions Paul at Coughlin High School circa 1978–82; the [yearbook index](https://coughlinhighschool.org/yearbooks.html) shows a 1982 yearbook, but no entry for him was checked. The 1972 Agnes flood and post-flood rebuilding are shown in [Wilkes University's annotated photograph tour](https://www.wilkes.edu/academics/library/agnes-flood-walking-tour/main-street.aspx). The 1982 Wilkes-Barre-area shooting discussed in the shared chat occurred **after** a normal spring graduation and at other locations, so it should not be described as a Coughlin school incident; absent a personal link it adds little to a family biography.

## Alexandria, Virginia

The [city's historical school study](https://alexandriava.gov/cultural-history/black-education-in-alexandria-a-legacy-of-triumph-and-struggle) describes unequal segregated systems and desegregation through the 20th century. It is crucial local context, yet no specific Weatherford or Smith school, race, or childhood period is established. The [city's historic map portal](https://alexandriava.gov/historic-alexandria/basic-page/historic-alexandria-maps) and [library research guide](https://alexandriava.gov/historic-alexandria/conducting-your-own-historic-research) can support an address-based study once the right family records are identified.
