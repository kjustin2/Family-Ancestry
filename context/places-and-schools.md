# Places, schools, and events

Place histories below are **settings**. Individual school claims are identified separately and tied to personal records.

## Siebeldingen, Rinnthal and the Palatinate

The [Siebeldingen municipality's school history](https://www.siebeldingen.de/dorfjubilaeum/schule-und-kirche.html) says the 1890 schoolhouse brought previously separate Protestant and Catholic schools under one roof. This suggests why an 18th-century ancestor's education cannot be inferred from today's elementary school. No enrollment, literacy, or school record has yet been found for Sebastian Disqué or his ancestors. A responsible answer to “how was my great-great-great grandfather's elementary education?” must first identify the person, place and childhood years; then seek church, school and local administrative records. Nearby school histories are only comparisons.

In the mill valleys, water power drove grinding equipment and mill rights could include grain rent, water dues and fishing rights. The specific [Geiselberger](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BChlen-u-m%C3%BChlorte/gau-bickelheim-glan/) and [Knittelsheim](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BChlen-u-m%C3%BChlorte/kleinottweiler-kusel/) records anchor that context far better than a generic picture of “old Germany.”

## Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania

The [city directories](https://pagenweb.org/~luzerne/towns/donnacd.htm) place candidate Kramers on Susquehanna and Prospect Streets across 1871–1914. These are **historical addresses**; a modern map pin or surviving building should not be treated as the original dwelling without parcel research. Wilkes-Barre's wider anthracite economy is documented by [Pennsylvania's mining history](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/dgs/documents/publications/documents/the%20pennsylvania%20manual%20volume%20127.pdf), but no record found here calls Matthew or Ferdinand a coal miner.

The [1889 directory](https://pagenweb.org/~luzerne/towns/donnacd.htm) lists Kate Kramer at 22 Susquehanna as a silk-mill beamer. [Pennsylvania Heritage](https://paheritage.wpengine.com/article/if-looms-could-speak-story-pennsylvanias-silk-industry/) explains how northeastern Pennsylvania silk mills employed women and girls in the coal region. The listing does not establish Kate's age, school history, wages, or which mill employed her.

Hilda Kramer Wolosz's [obituary](https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/citizensvoice/name/hilda-wolosz-obituary?id=7822560) reports G.A.R. Memorial High School, class of 1940. Mary Andrews Kramer's sampled [1950 census answer](https://1950census.archives.gov/iiif/2/1950census%2F43290879-Pennsylvania%2F43290879-Pennsylvania-135713%2F43290879-Pennsylvania-135713-0005.jpg/full/full/0/default.jpg) records **seventh grade completed**; it does not name her school or explain why formal schooling stopped then. The shared chat also mentions Paul at Coughlin High School circa 1978–82; the [yearbook index](https://coughlinhighschool.org/yearbooks.html) shows a 1982 yearbook, but no entry for him was checked. The 1972 Agnes flood and post-flood rebuilding are shown in [Wilkes University's annotated photograph tour](https://www.wilkes.edu/academics/library/agnes-flood-walking-tour/main-street.aspx). The 1982 Wilkes-Barre-area shooting discussed in the shared chat occurred **after** a normal spring graduation and at other locations, so it should not be described as a Coughlin school incident; absent a personal link it adds little to a family biography.

## Franconia, Fairfax County, Virginia

The family's three known addresses on **Colette and Miller Drives** use **Alexandria postal addresses** but sit in Fairfax County's **Franconia** area, as the [Census Geocoder](https://geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder/geographies/onelineaddress?address=6307%20Colette%20Dr%2C%20Alexandria%2C%20VA%2022315&benchmark=Public_AR_Current&vintage=Current_Current&format=json) shows for the anchor home. Fairfax County parcel histories independently place Gladys L. Smith and Garnett/Florence Weatherford on Miller Drive. The [1986 county plan](https://www.fairfaxcounty.gov/planning-development/sites/planning-development/files/assets/documents/comprehensiveplan/planhistoric/1986/area%204.pdf#page=64) describes Glynalta Park as an existing larger-lot subdivision. The [historic aerial viewer](https://www.fairfaxcounty.gov/maps/aerial-photography) can show how roads and housing developed over time.

**Family school accounts:** John attended **Edison High School**; Alice attended **Hayfield High School**. [FCPS lists Edison as opened in 1962](https://www.fcps.edu/sites/default/files/media/pdf/cafr2014.pdf). [Hayfield's school history](https://hayfieldss.fcps.edu/about/history) says its first classes began in 1969, initially on an afternoon shift at Edison because its new building was late. That is a striking connection between the schools but does not place either parent there in 1969; attendance years and yearbook entries are still needed. [Fairfax County maps](https://www.fairfaxcounty.gov/maps/) show **current** boundaries, which cannot prove historical attendance.

The independent City of Alexandria's [historic maps](https://alexandriava.gov/historic-alexandria/basic-page/historic-alexandria-maps) and [school-history research](https://alexandriava.gov/cultural-history/black-education-in-alexandria-a-legacy-of-triumph-and-struggle) describe the wider region. They should not be treated as the local government or school system for the Colette Drive property.

Garnett Weatherford Sr.'s documented **AB&W and Metro bus work** reached beyond his Fairfax neighborhood. The City of Alexandria's [short AB&W history includes a 1960s bus image](https://media.alexandriava.gov/docs-archives/historic/info/attic/2010/attic20101021bus.pdf), and [WMATA dates its takeover of AB&W to 4 February 1973](https://www.wmata.com/news/metrobus-50th-annivesary.html). These sources show his employer's setting, not his personal route or the exact bus he drove.
