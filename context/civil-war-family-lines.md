# Civil War question: did the Berwick and Blevins lines oppose each other?

**Answer so far:** A [family genealogy](https://cookancestry.com/web/glenn%20cook%20master%20file/2/22298.htm) calls Justin's **third-great-grandfather Gad Marshall Miller (1830–1904)** a Civil War soldier. The [1870 Sugarloaf Township census transcription](https://www.pa-roots.com/columbia/census/1870sugarloaftownship.html) places **Marshall**, wife **Elizabeth**, and two-year-old **Amos** together; Amos leads to Lester, Paul, Melissa, and Justin. Pennsylvania's [original Company K, 171st Infantry register](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/bah/dam/rg/di/r19-65registerpavolunteers/r19-65Regt171/r19-65Regt171%20pg%2053.pdf) records **Marshall Miller, 32**, enrolled 24 October 1862 and mustered 5 November. Its [facing remarks page](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/bah/dam/rg/di/r19-65registerpavolunteers/r19-65Regt171/r19-65Regt171%20pg%2054.pdf) says he mustered out with the company on 6 August 1863. The [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/civilwar/search-battle-units-detail.htm?battleUnitCode=UPA0171RIX) identifies the regiment as **Union drafted militia**.

**The identity gap matters:** the register gives the soldier's age but no home county, spouse, or children. Company K included men from other Pennsylvania counties, so the name and age do **not** by themselves prove the soldier was the Sugarloaf/Berwick father of Amos. The separate family genealogy says Gad served but does not display the military record it cites. Treat **Gad's service as a family-history lead** and the **171st identification as a plausible, unconfirmed match** until a pension file, 1890 veterans schedule, 1904 obituary, or service card supplies a connecting detail.

**Did they fight each other?** If the candidate records identify both ancestors, they served on **opposing sides**. That does not show they met in combat. The [171st Pennsylvania history](https://www.nps.gov/civilwar/search-battle-units-detail.htm?battleUnitCode=UPA0171RIX) places the regiment in Suffolk, Virginia, then New Bern and Little Washington, North Carolina, through mid-1863; the [61st North Carolina history](https://www.nps.gov/civilwar/search-battle-units-detail.htm?battleUnitCode=CNC0061RI) says it fought near Kinston before transfer to Charleston, South Carolina. The published unit summaries do not establish a shared battle. The 171st mustered out in August 1863, while the 61st continued into 1865. No individual battle participation has been confirmed for either man. Northern residence alone would not prove enlistment or a particular allegiance.

**Most useful next record:** Gad/Marshall's pension or 1890 veterans schedule naming a company and a Berwick/Sugarloaf residence or Elizabeth Hess; also read Shubill's full North Carolina roster entry at volume 14, page 740. Only after both identities and units are secure should battle reports be compared.
