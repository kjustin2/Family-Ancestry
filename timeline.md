# Family and economic timeline

This compares **documented work and assets**, not measured net worth. The German mill branch is one ancestral line among many; its property cannot be treated as wealth inherited by every later descendant.

| Period | People and place | Evidence about circumstances | Unknown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1684–1718 | Bernhard and Sebastian Disqué, Siebeldingen/Rinnthal | A specialist [history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BCllerfamilien/litera-d/) quotes Bernhard's offer to rebuild a ruined mill in 1684; Sebastian is later described as a master miller. | Income and school attendance. |
| 1718–1760s | Sebastian Disqué and Anna Barbara Schaaf, Palatinate | The [mill history](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BCllerfamilien/litera-d/) records their marriage and reports three hereditary leasehold mills associated with Sebastian and different sons. | Lease values, debts, and continuity to Magdalena. |
| 1768–1839 | Johann Ludwig Disqué and Knittelsheim successors | A 1768 [account](https://www.eberhard-ref.net/pf%C3%A4lzisches-m%C3%BChlenlexikon/pf%C3%A4lzische-m%C3%BChlen-u-m%C3%BChlorte/kleinottweiler-kusel/) describes two waterwheels; a 1788 inventory lists a mill, fields and meadows. | 1939–2023 | Ferdinand Francis Kramer | His [obituary](https://www.legacy.com/person/ferdinand-francis-kramer-%28fred%29-52588560) names parents Ferdinand **Louis** Kramer and Mary Andrews, Army Reserves service, and work at Techneglas. | Whether his father is chart's Ferdinand born 1913. |
| Recent generations | Justin/Paul and Ashley/Weatherford/Smith | Family-supplied identities only. | Work, education, finances, and older Weatherford/Smith generations need uniquely identifying records. |

**Economic reading:** Mill operation indicates access to valuable productive equipment or leases, but neither the asset list nor later records here yields a defensible monetary estimate. The Knittelsheim operation's documented succession to particular sons/brothers is one possible reason the asset did not follow every descendant. There is **no evidence of a family fortune being lost**, nor proof that Magdalena's line owned that mill. Wilkes-Barre directories support a later laboring/ropemaking setting, not a quantified fall in wealth.
