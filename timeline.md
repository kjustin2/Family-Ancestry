**Economic reading:** Mill operation indicates access to valuable productive equipment or leases, but neither the asset list nor later records here yields a defensible monetary estimate. The Knittelsheim operation's documented succession to particular sons/brothers is one possible reason the asset did not follow every descendant. There is **no evidence of a family fortune being lost**, nor proof that Magdalena's line owned that mill. Wilkes-Barre directories show several occupations at one address, including women's book and clothing work. The 1940 census adds one concrete property and wage snapshot: the older Ferdinand reported an owned **$2,400 home** and **$1,996 wages in 1939**; a resident son reported **$1,234 wages**. These are historical census responses, not household net worth or proof of assets transferred to descendants. In 1950 younger Ferdinand worked at a wire-rope works; Mary's census entry preserves a dress-factory occupation but reports housework that week and no 1949 wages. The older widowed Ferdinand shared a household with his employed son-in-law and daughter.
